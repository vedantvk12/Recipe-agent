# Culinary Scaling Rules and Ratios

## Section 1: Non-Linear Scaling for Leavening and Spices
When scaling recipes up or down, direct linear multiplication does not apply universally to all ingredients.
- Leavening Agents: Chemical leaveners like baking powder and baking soda should be scaled by the square root of the scale factor when scaling recipes greater than 4x original yield to prevent metallic aftertaste and collapsed crumb structures.
- Strong Spices and Salt: Capsaicin (chili powder, cayenne) and pungent seasonings (cloves, nutmeg, salt) concentrate rapidly. For scaling factors greater than 3x, apply a 0.8 scaling coefficient to total spices and adjust to taste during final preparation.
- Active Dry Yeast: Yeast reproduces during fermentation. When tripling or quadrupling dough batches, increase yeast by 1.5x to 2x rather than 3x or 4x to avoid over-proofing.

## Section 2: Evaporation and Liquid Reductions
Liquid evaporation depends on vessel surface area rather than fluid volume alone.
- Soups and Stocks: In double or triple batches, wide stockpots increase surface evaporation. Keep liquid scaling at 0.9x when simmering uncovered for over 45 minutes.
- Pan Sauces and Glazes: Cooking down sauces to a nappe consistency requires maintaining heat output. Never quadruple pan sauce liquids without increasing saucepan diameter proportionately.
